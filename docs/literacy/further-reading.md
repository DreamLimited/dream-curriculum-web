# Literacy — Further Reading (Official Sources)

*The official federal sources, consolidated. These are the places the facts in this curriculum come from — and the places you go when a fact matters. One-line notes on what each is for. Accuracy is the point of this layer (per the repo's own rule: if you are not certain of a fact, state it generally rather than inventing specifics).*

> **A note on websites.** Interfaces, URLs, and portal names change — that is the *tools* layer ([doctrine/08](../doctrine/08-tools-change-concepts-dont.md)). The *institutions* these sites represent endure: the registry, the bulletin boards, the rules, the published record. If a link moves, search the agency name — you will find the institution.

## The bulletin boards and the registry

| Source | What it is for | When you need it |
|---|---|---|
| **SAM.gov** — sam.gov | The System for Award Management: the master registry (UEI, CAGE, SAM registration) and the central bulletin board for federal **contract opportunities and contract awards**. | Registering your entity; finding contract RFPs; seeing who won and for how much. |
| **Grants.gov** — grants.gov | The central portal for federal **grant opportunities** — where NOFOs are published and (for most federal grants) where applications are submitted. | Finding a NOFO; reading a program's eligibility, review criteria, and deadlines; applying. |
| **USAspending.gov** — usaspending.gov | The public record of **what the government actually spends** — trace a dollar from appropriation to a specific award, to an agency, to a contractor. | The money-flow map ([where-the-money-flows](where-the-money-flows.md)) made real; post-award research; market intelligence. |

## The rules

| Source | What it is for | When you need it |
|---|---|---|
| **acquisition.gov (FAR)** — acquisition.gov | The **Federal Acquisition Regulation** — the rulebook for how the government buys things, plus agency supplements (DFARS for DoD, and others). | Checking the actual text behind an RFP's clauses; understanding why a requirement exists. |
| **OMB / Grants.gov policy pages** | The grants-policy framework — the rules for assistance dollars (2 CFR and OMB circulars) that sit behind every NOFO. | Understanding the *other* rulebook — the grants side of the transaction. |

## The small-business universe

| Source | What it is for | When you need it |
|---|---|---|
| **SBA (Small Business Administration)** — sba.gov | The agency that runs the small-business programs: **size standards**, NAICS guidance, and the **certifications** (HUBZone, 8(a), WOSB/VOSB). | Confirming a size standard; understanding what a certification requires; applying for or maintaining a certification. |
| **SBIR.gov / STTR** — sbir.gov | The Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs: **topics, solicitations, awards, and policies** for the research set-aside programs. | Finding SBIR/STTR topics (like the one in the [worked exemplar](../practice/worked-exemplar-day-in-the-life.md)); seeing Phase I/II/III awards; reading the program rules. |

## Worth knowing, one level down

| Source | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **GSA eLibrary** — gsa.gov/elibrary | The catalog of **GSA Schedule / MAS** contracts and the contractors on them — the vehicle landscape for repeated purchases. |
| **FPDS (Federal Procurement Data System)** — via USAspending.gov | The raw **contract award data** — the published record of who won what (USAspending is the friendly window on it). |
| **Agency forecasts** (DoD, GSA, and others) | Each large agency's **forecast of planned purchases** — the market *before* the solicitation drops. |

## How to use this list

1. **For the course and the practice layer**, these sources are where "real, published and closed" solicitations come from. Pick one from SAM.gov (an RFP) or Grants.gov (a NOFO) and work the [practice bank](../practice/exercises.md) on it.
2. **For a fact you need to be sure of** (a size standard, a certification rule, a deadline), go to the official source rather than a third-party summary. The literacy layer is accurate, but official sources are the ground truth.
3. **For the weekly habit**, USAspending.gov is the most underrated tool in this discipline: ten minutes a week reading published awards in your NAICS codes teaches you the competitive landscape faster than any newsletter.

## The one-sentence summary

The institutions — the registry, the bulletin boards, the rules, the published record — endure even as the websites change. Learn the institutions; the websites are just the current address ([doctrine/08](../doctrine/08-tools-change-concepts-dont.md)).
