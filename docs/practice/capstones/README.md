# Capstone Projects — Sample Program & Assessment Framework

*The capstone is where the doctrine stops being read and becomes done. It is the final assessment of every tier — the moment a learner takes a real pursuit, runs it through the pipeline, and defends the result before people who are paid to be skeptical. This folder is the sample program: how to run a capstone, how to grade it, and a worked sample capstone for every rung of the ladder.*

> **What this folder is, and is not.** The assignment briefs for the degree tiers live in [`course/`](../../course/): the shared capstone ([`course/capstone-build-an-orbital.md`](../../course/capstone-build-an-orbital.md)), the undergraduate extension ([`course/undergraduate-480-capstone-build-an-orbital.md`](../../course/undergraduate-480-capstone-build-an-orbital.md)), the graduate culminating experience (GC 690 / GC 690T, [`course/graduate-program-overview.md`](../../course/graduate-program-overview.md)), and the doctoral dissertation sequence (DL 903 / DL 990, [`course/doctoral-program-overview.md`](../../course/doctoral-program-overview.md)). The files in *this* folder are **sample projects** — complete, worked model answers and program-level guidance — so students can see the shape of excellence and instructors can see how to run and grade a capstone program across all three tiers. Read the brief and the sample together: the brief says what to do, the sample shows what done looks like.

## How to run a capstone program

A capstone is a program, not an assignment. It needs a calendar, a rulebook, a review mechanism, and a defense. The doctrine's own governance discipline supplies the shape: **criteria written before the work begins** (doctrine/04).

### The five rules that make a capstone honest

1. **Real documents, safe exercises.** Every capstone is built on a **real, published, closed** solicitation — never a live one. Nothing a student produces may bear on an active competition. The sample projects in this folder use *fictional* agencies and requirements precisely so they can be worked in a classroom without any risk of touching the real market; a student's own capstone should use a real closed solicitation (SAM.gov, Grants.gov, or the SBIR/STTR portals) unless the instructor explicitly chooses the fictional sample as the teaching object.
2. **The rubric is public.** The 4-level scale below is published before the first class. Students see exactly how their work is judged before they do it — the doctrine's gate discipline applied to grading.
3. **The defense is real.** Every tier ends in a **defense before a skeptical panel** — instructor, practitioners, and (where possible) an outside reader. The defense is where the doctrine is tested under questioning, and where a student who knows the words but not the discipline is separated from one who can do the work.
4. **Criteria before the call.** Where a capstone asks for a bid/no-bid call, a price, or a research claim, the criteria for the call are written **in advance** and graded before the answer is known. A gate charter graded in Week 3, judged against the call made in Week 12, is the whole discipline in miniature.
5. **Integrity is a skill, not a rule.** No fabricated evidence, no invented awards, no misstated past performance. The post-mortem is graded on honest comparison, not on being right. A capstone that fabricates fails the assessment outright — the same standard the federal market enforces on everyone who touches it.

### The stack-agnostic pledge

Every capstone at every tier obeys doctrine/08: **concepts are durable, tools are swappable.** Students may use paper, a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, or any word processor. A deliverable may *never* depend on a specific product or system, and no capstone requires knowledge of any particular software. When a sample project mentions a tool of any kind — a scoring sheet, a compliance tracker, a review log, a data store — it means *your organization's tooling*, whatever that is, and the concept is what is being tested. If a capstone deliverable would break because a vendor changed its product, the deliverable is testing the wrong thing.

### Program formats that work

The same five capstones run at different cadences. Match the format to the learner:

| Format | Cadence | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **14-week semester** | One milestone per week, defense in Week 14 | Degree-track students (B.S., M.S.) | The default; the undergraduate and graduate samples are built on it |
| **Compressed bootcamp** | 4–6 weeks, two milestones per week | Fellowship cohorts, continuing-education | Cuts the drafting weeks, keeps the gates and the defense |
| **Thesis / dissertation arc** | 6–24 months | M.S. thesis, doctoral dissertation | The doctoral sample is built for this; the research design *is* the milestone sequence |
| **Lifetime portfolio** | Years, revisited at each tier | Practitioners and alumni | The portfolio sample; the arc is the capstone |

### Teams vs. individuals

- **Undergraduate:** teams of 3–4, each member owning at least one deliverable; the defense is individual.
- **Graduate:** individual or small team, with a named capture lead who *owns the whole pursuit*; the defense is individual.
- **Doctoral:** individual by definition — a dissertation is a solo contribution, defended publicly.
- **Portfolio:** individual, with a committee that includes a practitioner mentor.

### The panel

Every defense panel should include at least one **practitioner** (someone who has sat in a source-selection or bid/no-bid room) and at least one **faculty or research reader** (someone who will test the *reasoning*, not the jargon). The panel's job is the doctrine's job: push until the student names the limits, then judge whether the work is *defensible*, not perfect.

## The common assessment rubric

Every capstone in this folder is graded on the same four-level scale. The scale is deliberately coarse — four levels force a decision and discourage the "everyone gets a B" drift that destroys grading as a governance mechanism.

### The four levels

| Level | Meaning | What the work looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Not Ready** | The deliverable does not yet do its job | Structure missing or wrong; facts wrong or fabricated; no visible doctrine; a reader cannot act on it |
| **Developing** | The shape is right, the execution is partial | Right structure with thin content; some parts strong, several parts weak; doctrine used but superficially; a reader can follow but not rely on it |
| **Proficient** | The deliverable is professional and defensible | Correct structure, complete content, doctrine applied correctly; evidence cited for claims; a reader could act on it |
| **Distinguished** | The work a senior professional or a researcher would be proud of | Everything at Proficient plus: insight beyond the assignment, a hard problem met honestly, a defense that survives a hostile panel, and a lesson the field or the firm could use |

### The shared dimensions

Every tier-specific rubric in this folder is built from these six dimensions, weighted differently by tier:

| Dimension | What it tests | Not Ready | Proficient | Distinguished |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Domain mastery** | Can the learner read and operate the real documents and structures of the discipline | Misreads core documents; wrong structures | Reads the solicitation, the factors, the volumes correctly | Reads them with insight — sees the strategy inside the compliance |
| **Doctrine use** | Is the pipeline, the gate, the score, the ORBITAL, or the color sequence visibly doing the work | No visible doctrine | Doctrine applied correctly | Doctrine applied and *questioned* — the learner knows where it is right and where it is thin |
| **Evidence** | Are claims grounded in data, sources, or documented reasoning | Assertion with no source | Factors, prices, and calls argued with cited evidence | Evidence is weighted and triangulated; the learner names what the evidence cannot prove |
| **Governance** | Were the gates, criteria, owners, and reviews real — and written before the work | Gates absent or after-the-fact | Gates identified with criteria and owners | The gate charter is graded before the call; the learner is *bound* by it |
| **Artifacts** | Are the deliverables complete, coherent, and mutually consistent | Large omissions; volumes contradict each other | Complete and consistent; the four volumes tell one story | The artifacts tell one story and the learner can defend every seam |
| **Defense** | Can the learner survive a skeptical panel and own the limits | Evasive or unsupported | Clear and credible under questioning | Survives a hostile board; names the limits before the panel finds them |

### How to apply the scale per milestone

Each sample capstone includes a **per-milestone rubric**: for every week's deliverable, the criteria and what each level earns. Two practical rules for graders:

1. **Grade the milestone, not the trajectory.** A student who nails Week 4 but misses Week 9 gets credit for Week 4 and a developing on Week 9 — the capstone is a pipeline, and a weak stage downstream is a real defect, not a rounding error.
2. **The composite is the capstone.** Weight the milestones to match the tier's priorities (the undergraduate sample weights the ORBITAL and the score; the graduate sample weights the capture plan and the price; the doctoral sample weights the design and the ethics plan), then convert the composite to the same four levels. The panel's defense grade is the final gate — it can move the composite by one level in either direction, exactly as a real readiness gate can stop a submission.

## How the tiers connect: the one arc

The four sample capstones are not four separate assignments; they are **four renderings of one arc** — the discipline from first read to first research contribution:

```
 Undergraduate        Graduate             Doctoral            Portfolio
 ────────────         ────────             ─────────           ─────────
 read → score         lead → govern        question → test     steward → teach
 build the ORBITAL    win the competition  prove the doctrine  see the whole arc
 CONSUME              APPLY                GENERATE            STEWARD
```

The undergraduate capstone proves the learner can *do* a pursuit. The graduate capstone proves the learner can *lead* a pursuit as a business. The doctoral capstone proves the learner can *study* the pursuit as a science. The portfolio capstone holds all three together over a career — the same person, the same doctrine, twenty years deeper. The [`portfolio-capstone.md`](portfolio-capstone.md) sample explains the arc; each tier's sample shows its own rung.
